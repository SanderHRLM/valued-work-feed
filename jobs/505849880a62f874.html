Engineering at Monizze

Our engineering organisation is built around agentic coding : our engineers don’t just write code, they lead their agents. Skills, agents and context files are versioned and reviewed with the code. Our standards and Architecture Decision Records are written for people and agents alike. Engineers join each initiative at discovery, alongside Product and UX, and them own its design, build, testing and after-care. Two engineering teams are each led by an Engineering Manager, who coaches +/-7 engineers, makes sure the quality holds and drives agentic adoption engineer by engineer. The Delivery Manager makes sure that what we commit to ships on time and as pitched. The Lead Architect owns the architecture direction and the ADRs and sets the framework for agentic coding. Principal Engineers act as the referents for each stack. One quality bar applies to everyone: be proud of what you ship, and AI-generated code meets the same standard as any other code.

The role

You make sure Monizze's software evolves in one coherent direction, written down and usable by humans and agents alike.

You lead through influence: you stay hands-on, close to the code and the engineers, and your authority comes from the quality of your decisions, the clarity of the standards and the trust you earn.

What You’ll Do

Keep the architecture coherent across products

  • carry the target architecture (multi-product, shared foundations) and make it understandable to everyone, not only to the people who drew it 

  • own the cross-cutting topics: API contracts, gateway and partner API versioning, orchestration vs. choreography, new service vs. new domain 

  • join discovery when an initiative goes beyond our agreed ecosystem or spans several stacks, so structural choices are known before the pitch 

  • surface structural projects early, and bring "done is done" discipline to migrations: frozen scope, explicit go, a closing criterion 

  • take on critical pieces yourself when complexity or urgency calls for it, including during incidents 

Drive architecture decisions

You turn structural choices into Architecture Decision Records (ADRs), keep that base current with the help of Principal Engineers, and make sure every team and agent follows it. You design how each decision is enforced; Engineering Managers check conformity in their teams. 

  • spot early where a structural choice is needed and propose the decision 

  • maintain two sets of ADRs: a short, curated Target that every new component must follow, and a Baseline for existing code that grows one agreed decision at a time 

  • run the ADR lifecycle, from proposal and review with the relevant engineers to decision and publication , and superseding with a new ADR when a decision changes 

  • attach an enforcement mechanism to every ADR (CI, agent skill or PR review) and have every repo declare which rules apply, so CI and agents load the right ones 

  • build consensus among engineers and, when they can't agree, frame the options for the CTO to decide 

Turn tech watch into decisions

Tech watch means turning what’s relevant into concrete proposals.

  • keep a living tech radar (Adopt / Trial / Assess / Hold), the entry point for any new technology discussion

  • run timeboxed experiments with a clear question: successes become an ADR, a standard or a skill, failures get documented too

  • bring in external experts when useful, and make sure their findings end in a decision, not a report

Lead the shift to agentic coding

Move the team from developers who write code to engineers who lead their agents. You set the direction and the framework; the Principal Engineers build the skills, agents and tooling within it.

  • define and regularly revisit the approach: what agents are used for, how, and with which guardrails on critical code (security, payments, card data) 

  • make the standards agent-ready and sponsor the CI steps and review agents that check PRs against them 

  • make the skills and agents landscape converge (shared vs. repo-specific) without killing bottom-up initiative 

  • drive adoption through demos, pairing and onboarding from day one, and track a few meaningful indicators to adjust the course  

Lead the architecture community

  • run workshops, reviews and decision sessions with Principal Engineers and Software Engineers: the standards are theirs, you make them happen 

  • explain the why behind decisions, not only the what, to Product, leadership and business stakeholders 

  • work closely with the CTO, the Delivery Manager and the Engineering Managers to follow-up on architecture risks, experiments and tech debt

What this role doesn’t hold

  • you don't manage people: coaching and performance sit with the Engineering Managers 

  • you don't own delivery, feature design or product priorities. You review and challenge, you don't take over 

  • you don't have the final say on contested decisions: the CTO arbitrates 

  • you're not a gatekeeper on every PR. Within the standards, engineers decide 

What stays yours: whether structural choices are made before the code, whether disagreements actually get decided, and whether engineers see you as a multiplier, not a bottleneck.
