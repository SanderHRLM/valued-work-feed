Businesses control team spend, issue unlimited virtual corporate cards with real controls, pay bills, and earn cash back on everyday expenses, all without leaving the bank they already use. The role

At Fluz, an account manager is called a Sidekick. You get a named book of corporate spending accounts, you carry a revenue number on it, and you’re expected to grow it.

These are businesses that buy at real volume across procurement, media buying, travel, gifting and inventory. Some arrived as small teams and grew into it; others were already at scale when they signed. Either way, growth here comes from expansion rather than new logos. An account that starts with one department buying gift cards can become five departments running card spend, travel and cardholders across the business. Finding and unlocking that is most of the job.

Part of it is making sure a large order never fails for a preventable reason. When a customer plans a six-figure purchase, funding, limits and timing have to be right in advance. Getting that wrong once costs more trust than a quarter of good service earns back.

You’re also how new things reach the market. We ship constantly: new products, new merchants and deeper rates, new controls, new ways to fund and pay. None of it matters until customers actually use it. When something launches, you’re expected to know it is cold, work out which accounts in your book it’s right for, and go sell it in. Not announce it. Sell it.

What you’ll do

  • Own a named book of corporate accounts and act as their operator, not their ticket queue
  • Monitor spend daily by account and by merchant, and chase declines and dips before the customer notices them
  • Maintain rate agreements: renegotiate as volume changes, handle reconciliations and payouts, and kill rates that are underwater
  • Manage limits, funding capacity and cut-off timing so a large planned order clears the first time
  • Expand accounts: more of the product (procurement through gift cards, bill pay, virtual bank accounts, travel card), more departments, more cardholders, more merchants
  • Take everything we launch to your book: know what’s shipping, work out which accounts it’s right for, and sell it in rather than announce it
  • Handle exceptions with evidence and a recommendation: refunds, capture discrepancies, failed transactions, returns, fee waivers
  • Run quarterly business reviews covering spend, earn delivered, rate performance and what’s coming
  • Bring back what you learn: what landed, what didn’t, and what your customers keep asking for that we don’t have yet
  • Keep account records current on every rate, exception and commitment

What we’re looking for

  • 3+ years in B2B account management carrying a revenue number
  • Experience with accounts that spend at scale; you should be comfortable when a single transaction is larger than some people’s quota
  • Detail-level command of the product. When a large order fails, the customer calls you, and you should be able to answer
  • Strong commercial instinct on rate versus margin. You can tell when a customer is costing you money, and you act on it
  • Skill at multi-stakeholder relationships across accounts payable, procurement, finance and operations
  • Numerate and organized. You’ll be reconciling real dollars
  • Someone who goes looking for expansion rather than waiting for the customer to ask

Bonus points

  • You’ve managed corporate card, expense management or corporate gifting programs
  • You’ve handled payment operations issues: chargebacks, returns, settlement timing, funding cut-offs
  • You’ve run QBRs that customers actually found useful

How you’ll be measured

Month-over-month and year-over-year spend growth on your book. Net revenue after rate cost. Logo and dollar retention. Expansion into new merchants, products and departments.

Why this role

Corporate Card is the line with the clearest runway. The product is built, the customers are real businesses with real budgets, and there’s meaningful unclaimed expansion sitting inside the existing base. You’d be early enough to take a lot of it.
